How Fledge works

From first paycheck to financially confident in one year.

Fledge is built for the moment money gets real: your first salary, your first lease, your first benefits enrollment form. Instead of a 400-page personal finance book, you get a guided path that fits into ten minutes a week and uses your actual numbers. Every lesson ends with one small action, so you finish the year with skills and results: an emergency fund, a captured 401(k) match, and a budget you actually follow.

The journey

Four steps, one habit.

Connect

Enter your take-home pay and fixed bills. Fledge builds a starting budget in under two minutes; no bank login required.

Learn

Unlock one five-minute module a week. Each one is plain-English, quiz-checked, and tied to a decision you're facing right now.

Practice

Turn lessons into action with auto-save challenges like "Round-Up Week" or "No-Spend Weekend," and earn feathers for streaks.

Grow

Track your net worth and hit milestones: $1K safety net, full employer match, first index fund, debt-free date.

Why we built it

Financial confidence shouldn't depend on who raised you.

Plenty of people start their careers without anyone at home who could explain a 401(k) or a credit score. Fledge exists to close that gap with a coach in your pocket that never judges, never sells you a product, and always shows the math.

We focus on the first year of work because the habits formed in that year tend to stick. Automating savings early, capturing an employer match, and avoiding high-interest debt can be worth hundreds of thousands of dollars over a career.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Do I need to link my bank account?

No. Fledge works with the numbers you type in. Bank linking is optional in the Flight plan for automatic tracking.

Is Fledge a financial advisor?

No. Fledge is an education tool. It explains concepts and runs illustrative math, but it never recommends specific securities. For personal investment decisions, talk to a licensed professional.

Who is Fledge for?

Anyone in their first few years of full-time work: new grads, career switchers, and anyone who never got "the money talk."
